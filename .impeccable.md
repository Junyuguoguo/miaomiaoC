## Design Context

### Users
Students use the system to practice programming questions, join online exams, review results, and decide whether to upgrade to VIP access. Teachers and administrators use it as an operational console for exams, question banks, violations, statistics, and VIP pricing/contact configuration.

### Brand Personality
Calm, precise, trustworthy. The product should feel like a dependable exam workspace rather than a marketing site: clear hierarchy, stable controls, readable tables, and low-friction workflows.

### Aesthetic Direction
Use a light, data-dense dashboard style with quiet surfaces, crisp borders, restrained shadows, and a blue/green status palette. Keep the interface technical enough for programming education, but avoid generic AI-looking purple gradients, floating blobs, over-rounded glass cards, and decorative copy.

### Design Principles
- Prioritize repeated-use efficiency: navigation, tables, filters, exam cards, and forms must be easy to scan.
- Make VIP status and upgrade actions visible without interrupting ordinary study workflows.
- Use semantic color roles for primary actions, success, warning, danger, and VIP accents.
- Keep motion purposeful and subtle; no decorative animation that competes with exam focus.
- Preserve accessibility basics: clear labels, visible focus states, touch-friendly controls, and readable contrast.
